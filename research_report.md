# Research report — "Falklands War: 74 Days" (YouTube Short)

**Research date:** 8 November 2026. Historical events (1982) are kept separate from current events (2013, 2026).

## Why this story
Two countries on opposite sides of the Atlantic, a small but very recognisable island group, a clear sequence of dated events, and a map-friendly narrative (invasion → task force → landing → surrender). Every beat is dated and cross-checked, which makes it suitable for accurate animation.

## Countries and geography
- **United Kingdom** (UK) and **Argentina**. Disputed territory: **Falkland Islands / Islas Malvinas** (and South Georgia). The UK administers them as a British Overseas Territory; Argentina claims sovereignty. The video uses both names.
- Britannica gives the islands as about **300 mi (480 km)** east of Argentina's coast. (Note: Natural Earth geometry shows Argentine territory in Tierra del Fuego/Isla de los Estados is closer, ~360 km; the 480 km figure refers to the mainland/Patagonian coast as commonly cited. The video says "about 480 km … from Argentina's coast".)
- London → Stanley geodesic distance, computed on the WGS84 ellipsoid from the coordinates used in the map: **12,655 km** (shown as "12,700 km"; narration says "over twelve thousand kilometres").

## Verified timeline (1982 — historical)
| Date | Event | Sources |
|---|---|---|

| 2 Apr 1982 | Argentina invades the Falklands (Operation Rosario) | Britannica; Falklands National Archives |
| 3 Apr 1982 | Argentina invades South Georgia | Falklands National Archives |
| 5 Apr 1982 | Task force (carriers HMS Hermes and Invincible) sails from Portsmouth | Royal Naval Association; London Gazette; Royal Navy Museums |
| Apr 1982 | Ascension Island used as the staging base | RNA; Royal Navy Museums; Semaphore (RAN) |

| 21 May 1982 | British landing at San Carlos Water | Falklands National Archives |
| 14 Jun 1982 | Argentine commander Gen. Menéndez surrenders at Stanley; "74 days" of conflict | Falklands National Archives; multiple summaries |

## Casualties (historical)
- Common figures: **255 British**, **649 Argentine** servicemen killed, **3 Falkland Islanders**.
- Figures vary slightly by source (British 255–258; Argentine 649–655). The video uses 255 / 649 / 3 and carries an on-screen note "Figures vary slightly by source".

## Later events
- **10–11 Mar 2013 referendum (historical):** 99.8% voted to remain a British Overseas Territory, turnout 92%, 3 votes against (Reuters, BBC/RNZ, Al Jazeera). Argentina rejected the poll's relevance.
- **2026 (current, verified 8 Nov 2026):** Argentina continues to assert sovereignty and has called for renewed negotiations (Reuters, 24 Apr 2026). The UK rejects the claim and says islanders have the right to decide their future. In Sep–Oct 2026 the dispute escalated around an offshore oil project (AP, Al Jazeera 29 Sep 2026). The video states only the stable core: *Argentina still claims the islands; Britain says the islanders decide; the dispute is not settled.* Rapidly-changing details were deliberately left out.

## Illustrative (not literal) graphics — disclosed
- Invasion arrow: runs from the Argentine coast towards Stanley to show direction only, not the actual fleet route.
- Task-force route: smoothed path Portsmouth → Ascension → Falklands, labelled "illustrative route" on screen.
- British advance arrow (San Carlos → Stanley): shows the general direction of advance only.
- Territory fill: the Falklands/South Georgia fill colour switches to Argentina's colour during the occupation and back at the British recapture (2–3 April → 14 June / 26 April).

## Not claimed
No troop numbers, ship counts, or battle-by-battle positions are shown, because they could not be verified at map-level precision in this production.
